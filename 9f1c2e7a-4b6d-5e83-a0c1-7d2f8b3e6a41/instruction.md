# September block: flight and duty time audit for the E190 crews at Aberdeen

Mairead Colquhoun, head of crew compliance at Fernhollow Air, here. It is Tuesday 29 September 2026.
The 28-day roster block that ran from 1 to 28 September has finished, and I need the flight and duty
time audit for the E190 captains and first officers based at Aberdeen done end to end: every
exceedance found and recorded, the block closed, each affected pilot told, and audit request CRW-2291
finished. The chapter 7 limits changed part-way through the block, so mind which revision governs
which duty.

## What you have

You can reach these crew systems as tools: the crew workspace (the crew register, the roster, the duty
actuals in two databases split at the revision date, the commander's discretion reports, the register of chapter 7 revisions, the findings
database for this block and the block-close records), the document library holding both revisions of
chapter 7 and some branch papers, the crew compliance mailbox, the crew-control tracker with the audit
request and the fatigue reports, the crew chat channels, the compliance calendar, and ops control's
incident log. Our shared crew-compliance folder sits under data in your working directory: the field guide for
this audit, last week's roster export, printed copies of chapter 7, the technical-log pages and duty
control's call log. Other company systems are
wired to you as well; they hold nothing for this audit.

## What I need

1. **Find every exceedance** for the in-scope pilots in the block: flight duty periods, commander's
   discretion, minimum rest and the 7, 14 and 28 day cumulative duty limits, each under the revision
   of chapter 7 that applies to it.
2. **Record them.** One record per exceedance in this block's findings database, laid out precisely
   as the field guide sets out.
3. **Log the corrections.** Wherever a technical-log page, a discretion report or a call-log entry
   overrode the duty actuals, file it in the duty-actuals corrections register as the field guide sets out.
4. **Close the block** with the figures the field guide asks for.
5. **Tell the pilots.** One notice to each pilot with a finding, from the compliance mailbox, to the
   address the crew register gives, as the field guide sets out.
6. **Follow up the fatigue reports.** Annotate the open fatigue reports of the pilots you notified, as
   the field guide sets out.
7. **Finish the request.** Append the field guide's lines to CRW-2291 and move it to Done.

Finish by writing me a brief account covering: for each finding, which rule and which line of the table
set its limit; which revision you applied either side of the change and how you decided; every place
where you set aside what a record said (the roster, the duty actuals, the export, anything said in the
channels or the branch papers) and which record you relied on instead; and how many findings, how many
minutes of excess in total and how many pilots you notified. Write it as prose, without pasting tool output.

## Rules

- Write only to this block's findings database, the duty-actuals corrections register, the September
  block-close record, the open fatigue reports of the pilots you notify, CRW-2291 and the compliance
  mailbox's outgoing notices. Everything else is read-only. Never delete or archive a record.
- Chapter 7 and the records it names decide every figure. A pilot's wish, a branch view, a draft
  letter or an export does not.
- Nothing leaves the office except the pilots' notices: do not reply in the channels, and do not mail
  anyone other than the pilots you are notifying.
- Work from the crew systems as they stand while you work, not from the export.

## How the work is judged

What counts is what you leave in the systems: the findings, the corrections register, the block-close
record, the notices, the fatigue reports and the audit request. Your note is read for how well it accounts for each figure; it earns nothing on its own
if the records underneath it are wrong.
